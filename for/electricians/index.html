You are on a job. The phone rings. A burning smell or a sparking panel is a 911-first call, and it still has to be logged and dispatched.

The calls you are missing

For a electrician, the ones that get away are dead outlets, tripping breakers, panel work, burning smells and partial power loss. They come in while you are already working, and the homeowner does not leave a voicemail — they call the next shop in the results.

What picks up instead

It answers in your company name, only after your own line rings out. It gets the problem, gives the safety step — kill the breaker, and leave the house for anything burning or sparking — checks the zip is in your area, states your dispatch fee, and books into the arrival windows you actually run. You get a text with the name, address and the problem in their words.

It will not quote your work

It states your dispatch fee and nothing else. Pricing a electrical repair over the phone is how you end up arguing in somebody's basement, so it says the tech prices it on site. Every time.
