🏅 The offer — Founding Shops

Free is only powerful if it has a reason, a limit, and a price behind it. Never say "it's free" on its own — say this instead.

Say it exactly like this

I'm taking on 10 shops in Chester County as founding shops. You get the whole thing free — website, booking, the rewards card, all of it — and you keep founding pricing for as long as you stay. After the 10 it's $79/mo. I'd rather have you in the first ten than have your money.

What you ask for instead of money

Free is a trade, not charity — and asking makes it worth more, not less. Ask for all three, out loud, when they say yes:
1 · A Google review for you, once it's working.
2 · Permission to film it — their shop, their site, their reaction. That's your content for the week.
3 · Two barbers they know. "Who else should have this?" Ask while they're happy, not later.

Why a limit matters

Ten is not a marketing trick, it's the truth — you can only hand-hold about that many while you're learning what breaks. Say that. "I can only look after ten properly" is more believable than any countdown timer, and it is the reason the next shop should move now.

When the ten are gone

Stop giving it away and start naming the price on the walk-in. By then you have ten shops, real screenshots, a Google review and a win-back number you can quote — "the last shop pulled 6 regulars back in a week." That sentence is what you are actually building right now.

🏗️ The pre-built play — lead with this

This one kills "I'll think about it", because there's nothing left to think about — it already exists. Ten minutes at home, then walk in holding the finished thing.

1 · The night before

Open the pre-build tool. Search the shop on Google Maps, copy their name, address, phone, hours and prices into it, pick a look, tap Build. Their site is live before you've met them. Load it on your phone before you walk in.

2 · At the door

Looked your shop up on Google — you've got no website on your listing, so people searching "barber near me" are finding everyone but you. So I built you one. That's it — it's live right now.

3 · Hand them the phone and stop talking

Their name. Their prices. Their hours. Open now. The site does the pitch — anything you say past this point can only lose it.

4 · The question that closes it

Before you talk price, ask this and then be quiet and let him answer. He will start counting in his head, and the number will bother him:

How many guys were coming to you every few weeks last year that you haven't seen in a while?

5 · Show him Who's due

Open the demo shop's owner screen — the dashboard, code demo, PIN 1234 — and scroll to Who's due a cut. Hand him the phone. Then say it plain:

That's every regular who's stopped coming, oldest first. Tap a name and it opens your texts with the message already written. Five taps on a slow Tuesday is your afternoon.

6 · The ask

It's yours, free — you'd be one of my 10 founding shops. I'll leave it up either way. Want me to text you the keys so you can change anything you want?

7 · If they nod

The tool has the text pre-written with their sign-in keys — one tap sends it. Then ask the three founding-shop questions above: the review, the filming, and two barbers they know. Ask right now while they're happy.

🚪 The cold walk-in (no prep)

For when you're passing a shop you didn't prep. Thirty seconds, then the phone does the selling. Don't pitch past the handoff — let them play.

1 · Opener

Hey — you the owner? I'm Nick, I'm local. I built a rewards app for barbershops. It's free to start and takes about 30 seconds to set up. Can I show you on my phone real quick?

2 · Hand them the phone

Open thebarberloop.com/rewards/?c=demo first. Unlock your phone. Hand it over — don't hold it.

So this is what your customer sees. They scan your QR when they sit down, and every cut is a point toward a free one. See those games? They play while they wait and earn coins toward the free cut. Try one.

3 · The why (say it while they're playing)

Here's the real thing. Your regulars are one bad week from trying the shop down the street. This gives them a reason to come back to you — they've got points sitting here with your name on them. It's the same reason nobody switches coffee shops when they're three stamps from a free one.

4 · Close

Want me to set yours up right now? I just need your shop name and a 4-digit PIN. You'll have your own QR code before I leave.

5 · If they say yes

Hand them your phone on thebarberloop.com/rewards/signup/ — let them type their own name and PIN. It's theirs, not yours. Then: print their poster, put it on the counter, and add their first customer right there while you're standing in the shop.

🛡️ When they push back

Short answers. Agree first, then flip it. Never argue with a barber in his own shop.

💬 DMs & texts

Swap {shop} for their name. Send from your personal account, not a business page — barbers ignore business pages.

🎬 Content that pulls shops in

The arcade is the hook — no other barber software has games. Film the phone, not yourself. First 2 seconds decide everything.

🎨 Make the graphic for these

Shoot it once: film a real customer playing Barber Dash in the chair, cut it to 12 seconds, post the same clip to TikTok, Reels, and a barber Facebook group with three different hooks above.

🖨️ Leave-behind flyer

One page. Print a stack, leave one on the counter of every shop that says "not today" — half of them scan it that night.
