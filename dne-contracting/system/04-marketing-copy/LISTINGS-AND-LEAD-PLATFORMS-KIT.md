## Tier 1 — Free, set up in week 1 (about 2 hours total)

### Yelp for Business — yelp.com/claim
- Claim or create. Category: Plumbing + Contractors + Kitchen & Bath.
- Add 10+ photos, hours, the short description, "Women-owned" attribute (under "Business highlights"), "Free estimates", "Licensed".
- **Do not buy Yelp ads.** Their sales team will call repeatedly. Say no. Yelp ads are the worst ROI in home services.
- Yelp filters reviews from people who don't use Yelp regularly. Only ask for Yelp reviews from clients who already have Yelp accounts; everyone else → Google.
- Reply to every review. Same voice as Google.
- Expect: 1–3 leads/month passively once 5+ reviews.

### Nextdoor Business — business.nextdoor.com
- Free. This is the closest thing to her Facebook groups: neighborhood-based, women-heavy, recommendation-driven.
- Claim the page, add photos, short description, service area (pick every neighborhood in a 20-mile radius).
- Once a month, post the short Facebook post from `#playbook` as a Nextdoor business post.
- Ask 3 happy clients to "recommend" her on Nextdoor — recommendations there show up when neighbors ask "anyone know a plumber?"
- Expect: 2–5 leads/month, high trust, converts like referrals.

### Bing Places — bingplaces.com
- Free. Import directly from Google Business Profile (one click). Done in 5 minutes. Bing is ~10% of local searches and heavily used by 50+ homeowners.

### Apple Business Connect — businessconnect.apple.com
- Free. Apple Maps listing for every iPhone user who searches "plumber" in Maps or Siri. Claim, verify, paste short description, photos, hours. 15 minutes.

### Facebook Business Page — services section
- Already exists. Under "Services" add the 7 services from the GBP kit (no prices). Turn on "Book now" button → planner link. Turn on Messenger auto-reply with the DM 1 script.

### BBB — bbb.org/get-accredited (free listing; skip paid accreditation for now)
- Free profile exists automatically for most businesses; claim it and add correct info. Older homeowners check it. Accreditation ($400–800/yr) can wait until year 2.

## Tier 2 — Google Local Services Ads (turn on at 10+ Google reviews)

### What it is
The "Google Guaranteed" green-check ads that appear *above* everything else for "plumber near me". Pay per lead (a call or message), not per click. Google screens the business (license, insurance, background check) and shows the guarantee badge.

### Why wait
LSA ranks by review count, rating, and response speed. With 0–5 reviews she'll pay for leads and rank at the bottom. At 10+ reviews and 4.8+, it's the best paid channel in home services. Target: turn on in week 5–6.

### Setup (1–2 hours + 1–2 weeks verification)
1. ads.google.com/localservices → Get started.
2. Business info: use the exact GBP name/address/phone. Link to the existing GBP.
3. Services: Plumber category, then check: water heater, leak repair, fixture install, drain, pipe repair. Also apply under "Remodeling" if it's offered in her area (it varies by region).
4. Upload: PA HIC certificate, general liability insurance certificate (Google needs the certificate holder to be Google LLC — ask her insurance agent, it's a 5-minute change), owner ID for background check.
5. Background check: done by Evident/Pinkerton, 1–2 weeks, free.
6. Budget: start at $1,000/month, bid mode "Maximize leads". Expect $35–70 per lead for plumbing in Montgomery County.
7. Service area: 20 miles around Pottstown to start. Expand later.
8. Hours: set to when she'll actually answer. **Missed LSA calls are still charged and tank the ranking.** If she can't answer 8–6, set LSA hours narrower.
9. Turn on message leads as well as calls.
10. Ask for reviews *through LSA* too — LSA reviews count toward the badge score separately from GBP.

### Rules that keep it profitable
- Answer every LSA call within 3 rings or it goes to a competitor and she's charged anyway.
- Dispute junk leads (spam, wrong service, outside area) within 30 days — Google credits them.
- Every LSA lead gets typed into the planner so the nurture sequence runs.
- Check the LSA dashboard weekly: cost per lead, leads booked. If cost/lead > $90 for two weeks, lower the bid.

### Expect
15–25 leads/month at $1,500 budget, 35–45% book a consult. This becomes her #2 channel behind Facebook.

## Tier 3 — Pay-per-lead marketplaces (optional, week 8+, one at a time)

These sell the same lead to 3–5 contractors at once. They work for capacity-filling, not for building the brand. Her positioning (calm, no pressure) is actually an edge here because everyone else calls within 30 seconds and hard-sells.

### Angi (Angi Ads / Angi Leads, formerly HomeAdvisor) — pro.angi.com
- Angi and HomeAdvisor merged; one account now.
- **Free profile first** (Angi Ads free tier): claim, photos, description, reviews. Do this in week 1 — it's a listing.
- **Paid leads (Angi Leads):** $30–120 per lead, shared. Set a hard monthly cap ($300 to test). Pause anything that isn't converting in 30 days. Only turn on for "Bathroom remodel" and "Kitchen remodel" categories, not general plumbing — the plumbing leads are low-value and expensive.
- Response rule: call within 5 minutes, then follow the DM scripts by text.

### Thumbtack — thumbtack.com/pro
- Pay per lead only when a homeowner contacts *her* (better than Angi's shared model).
- Profile: short description, 10 photos, "women-owned" mention in the first line, response time matters for ranking.
- Set categories: Bathroom remodel, Kitchen remodel, Plumbing repair. Set max weekly budget $100 to test.
- Thumbtack customers price-shop. Her reply template (paste in Thumbtack's saved replies):
  > Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out. I don't quote without seeing the space — it's the only way the number is honest. I offer a free, no-pressure walk-through at your home; we'd talk options and you'd get a written estimate after. Would a weekday or weekend suit you better?

### Houzz — pro.houzz.com
- Free profile. Kitchen/bath focused, design-minded women homeowners, exactly her market. Upload every before/after as a "project" with a 2-sentence story. Do not buy Houzz Pro. Expect a slow trickle of high-value leads.

### Porch, Networx, CraftJack, Bark
- Skip. Low quality, aggressive billing, hard to cancel.

## Tier 4 — Trade and trust signals (once, cheap)

- **PA Attorney General HIC registration** — she must be registered (the "PA HIC #"). Verify at hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov and put the number on the site, GBP, Yelp, every ad. It's the law in PA for any home improvement over $500.
- **PHCC (Plumbing-Heating-Cooling Contractors Assoc.)** — optional, $300–500/yr, adds a badge.
- **Women's Business Enterprise (WBENC / PA WBE)** — optional certification; opens some commercial/municipal work later. Not now.
- **Chamber of Commerce (TriCounty Area Chamber, Pottstown)** — ~$300/yr, free directory listing + networking. Worth it in year 1 for referrals from realtors and property managers.
- **Realtor referral list** — not a platform, but the highest-value referral source in remodeling: 5 local realtors who get a $250 thank-you per closed project. Send them the "Meet me" video and the referral page.

## Where leads from each land
Every platform → she answers → books consult → **types the person into the website planner** (source dropdown: add `lsa`, `yelp`, `nextdoor`, `thumbtack`, `angi`, `houzz`, `realtor` to the `source` field in Supabase) → nurture runs → the 30-day funnel view shows which platform is actually paying.

## Monthly review (10 minutes, you)
Open Supabase `funnel_30d` grouped by source. Anything with cost > $150 per booked consultation for two months gets paused. Facebook and referrals will win; the job of the paid platforms is to fill gaps in the calendar, not to be the business.
