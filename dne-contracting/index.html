Why the consultation is different

Inviting someone into your home should never feel awkward, rushed, or uncomfortable.

If you've ever walked away from a contractor conversation feeling unheard, pressured, or talked down to, you're not alone. We hear it from women homeowners all the time. It's the reason a lot of kitchens and bathrooms stay the way they are for years.

So the consultation is built the other way round. You talk, we listen. Then we explain what's possible in your space, what it might cost, how long it takes, and whether we're the right fit. If we are, great. If we're not, you still walk away with clarity.

The visit

What the visit looks like

Written down so there are no surprises.

Set one up
  1. You pick the time. We take our shoes off.

    Evenings and Saturdays are fine. Even if you're just at the thinking stage.

  2. We walk the room together.

    You tell us what's bothering you and what you've been picturing. We mostly listen.

  3. We explain, plainly.

    Layout, plumbing, cabinets, tile, what's easy, what's not. If something's a bad idea for your house, we'll say so.

  4. We leave. You get numbers in writing.

    A few days later: what's included, what it costs, how long. Nothing hidden, nothing to sign that day.

The question everyone asks

What does it cost?

The ballpark ranges further down will get you in the right neighborhood. But a number picked before anyone has stood in your kitchen is a guess, and guesses either scare people off or disappoint them later.

What you get after the walk-through: a written estimate that lists what's included, what it costs, and how long it takes. If it's more than you'd hoped, we'll show you which parts are must-do and which can wait, or split it into phases.
