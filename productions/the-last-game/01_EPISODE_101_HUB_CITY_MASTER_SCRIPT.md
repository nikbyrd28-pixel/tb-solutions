## SCENE 1 — "THE TWO PHOTOGRAPHS" (0:00–0:45)
*The entire franchise thesis in 45 seconds. We open on the contradiction.*

🎵 Silence. Then a single low sub-bass drone fades up from nothing. One piano note.

🎬 BLACK. A single 35mm slide projector *ka-chunk*. A photograph slams onto screen: a young Black man in a crisp Versace suit, courtside at a Lakers game, laughing beside recognizable wealth. Everything gilded.

🔊 Projector *ka-chunk* + slide whir.
🎥 Slow 5-second push-in on his face. Locked, deliberate.
💡 Warm tungsten, halation blooming off the gold.

🎙️ (Chico, VO — calm, weathered) "This is me. Beverly Hills. I paid forty-eight thousand dollars for three suits that afternoon."

🔊 *KA-CHUNK.* Slide changes.

🎬 SECOND photograph slams in: a 15-year-old boy on cracked Compton pavement, cradling a friend's body under a freeway bridge, red spreading across a blue shirt. Sirens smearing in the far background.

💡 Cold, blue, sodium-vapor. The warmth is gone.
🎥 The push-in *reverses* — we pull back, small, helpless.

🎙️ (VO) "And this… is also me. Same person. Fifteen years apart." (beat) "The distance between these two photographs is the whole story. And somewhere in that distance — was the United States government."

🔊 Hard silence. Dice rattle *once*. Cut to black.

🔤 (typed, one word at a time, chalk-white): H O W ?

🧠 **Retention:** The "same person / two photos" contradiction is the strongest possible open — it forces the question the entire series answers. We state the CIA thesis in line one so the algorithm-scrubbing viewer knows the payoff exists.

💬 [Chico Brown] "Same person. Fifteen years apart."

💡 Left half warm/bright; right half cool/shadowed. The dividing line is a literal chain-link fence running down frame center.
🟦 A data-card slides up: *George H.W. Bush's second child was born in Compton — when the city had a white majority.*
🔊 A single archival newsreel voice ghosts under; then a record-scratch stop.

🎙️ (VO) "Here's something they don't teach you. A former President of the United States — his second child was born right here, back when Compton was a white-majority town. The same city that would later birth the Crips and the Pirus." (beat) "Same streets. Different Americas. What changed a place that fast?"

✂️ The chain-link fence "unzips" the frame open into the next scene.

## SCENE 22 — "THE INDICTMENT" (37:00–39:00)

🎬 1988. Chico is 24. A six-year FBI investigation lands. The indictment. Cold institutional world floods everything. Bail. A desperate flight to Washington, D.C. The inevitable return.

🎥 Full "power-world" grammar: locked, symmetrical, clinical. Fluorescent government interiors. The warmth is gone for good.
💡 Hard cold white; venetian-blind shadows; fluorescent flicker.
🔊 A typewriter/stamp *THUNK* on the indictment; handcuffs; a heavy door.

🎙️ (VO) "1988. I was twenty-four. And a six-year FBI investigation I never even felt… finally closed its fist." 

🟦 A document stamps across frame: *SEALED INDICTMENT — UNITED STATES v. …* then a redaction bar slides over the name.
🔊 Rubber stamp *THUNK*; the redaction slides with a mechanical hiss.

🎙️ (VO) "I made bail. And I did the dumbest, most human thing — I ran. Hid out in D.C., told myself I could outrun the federal government." (beat) "You can't. They brought me back to face the music."

🟦 A stark stat card, white on black: *Federal criminal cases end in conviction over 99% of the time. NO ONE BEATS THE FEDS.*
🔊 Silence on the card. One dice rattle.

🎙️ (VO) "Everybody around me took the plea. Decades, signed away. But I was young, and proud, and stupid enough to think I could beat a ninety-nine-percent machine." (beat) "So I went to trial."

✂️ Hard cut.

🧠 **Retention loop:** "NO ONE BEATS THE FEDS" set up as an immovable wall — so the next line (he DID) is a reward, and the FOLLOW-UP loop (they'll never let that stand) drives the season. The redacted-name graphic teases without over-claiming.
📼 B-roll: courthouse exteriors, federal seals, fluorescent hallways, handcuffs, D.C. skyline, a Greyhound/airport.
💬 [VO] "I was stupid enough to think I could beat a ninety-nine-percent machine."

## SCENE 23 — "ACQUITTED (THE FIRST CRACK)" (39:00–40:30) — **REWARD + THE BIG LOOP**

🎬 A courtroom. 1988–1991, rendered as a blur of anxiety — clocks, jurors' faces, sleepless nights — then a gavel, and the word.

🎥 Time-compression montage (ramped clocks, dissolving calendars), then a single locked, quiet shot for the verdict.
💡 Anxious flicker → then one clean beam of light finding Chico's face on "acquitted."
🔊 Ticking clocks layering into chaos → cut to silence → the gavel → a single exhale.

🎙️ (VO) "1988 to '91. Three years of anxiety. And then—" (beat) "Acquitted. Not guilty of drug conspiracy. I beat them." 

🟦 A newspaper-headline graphic spins up then freezes: *ACQUITTED.* The word almost glows.
🔊 A triumphant sting — but it's undercut immediately by the sub-bass drone returning.

🎙️ (VO) "I felt invincible all over again. Like I cheated fate twice." (beat, quiet, chilling) "But here's what I didn't understand yet. When you embarrass a ninety-nine-percent machine — it doesn't forget. It waits." (beat) "And it starts asking a different question. Not 'how do we convict him?' But — 'who was feeding him all along?'"

🎬 SLOW PUSH on a single, previously-ignored detail: a case file on the prosecutor's table, and clipped to it, a photograph of a smiling, pudgy Latino man. We hold on the face. We don't name him.

🔤 *He beat the Feds. That was his biggest mistake.*
🟦 The red surveillance dot returns — blinking now over that photograph.

🧠 **Retention REWARD + master loop:** the acquittal pays off "NO ONE BEATS THE FEDS," then instantly re-weaponizes it. Pushing in on the unnamed Blandon photo is the direct hand-off to Ep 102/103 — the audience now NEEDS to know who that man is. This is the franchise's central hook made physical.
📼 B-roll: courtroom clocks, calendar pages, newspaper presses, a gavel, a case file, a photo paperclip.
💬 [VO] "It doesn't forget. It waits."
