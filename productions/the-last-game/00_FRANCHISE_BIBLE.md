# THE LAST GAME — FRANCHISE BIBLE
### The Balance of Power Between the CIA & Compton
*A cinematic educational documentary franchise — adapted from the memoir by Chico Brown & Jay Burton*

---

## 1. THE ONE-LINE PROMISE (the whole franchise in a sentence)

> A kid from Compton rose from a dice game under a bridge to shaking hands with billionaires — and along the way he discovered the same government that jailed him may have flooded his neighborhood with the very drug that destroyed it.

That single contradiction — **victim and kingpin, hunted and connected, Compton and the CIA** — is the engine. Every episode pulls one more thread out of it.

---

## 2. WHY THIS IS A FRANCHISE, NOT A FILM

This is not a gang story. It is a **power story** — a systems story disguised as a memoir. The audience thinks they're watching a redemption arc; they're actually being taught how power, money, policy, and race collided in one American decade and reshaped a city forever.

**The educational spine (what the viewer secretly learns):**
- How a neighborhood becomes an economy.
- How the crack epidemic was manufactured, distributed, and policed.
- What Iran-Contra actually was — explained through one man who lived downstream of it.
- How mass incarceration was engineered through sentencing law.
- How redemption, media, and money can rewrite a life.

**The emotional spine (what keeps them watching):**
Grief → ambition → power → betrayal → revelation → reckoning → redemption.

Each episode = one chapter of the education + one turn of the emotion + one unanswered question that forces the next click.

## 4. TONE & REFERENCES ("what does it feel like?")

Blend of:
- **Netflix docu-drama energy** — *Wild Wild Country*, *The Last Dance*, *Dope Sick*
- **Investigative gravity** — *The Jinx*, *American Nightmare*
- **Street cinematography** — *Snowfall*, *Menace II Society*, Hype Williams saturation
- **MrBeast retention pacing** — no dead air, a promise every 20 seconds
- **Hollywood storyboard discipline** — every frame is designed, never grabbed

**Rule of the room:** We never glamorize the drug trade. We *seduce with it, then bill the audience for it.* Every high is followed by its cost within the same act.

---

## 5. VISUAL LANGUAGE (the look bible)

**Two-world color system** — the entire franchise lives on a color contrast:

- **COMPTON / THE STREETS:** warm sodium-vapor amber, deep crushed blacks, teal shadow, film grain, halation on every light source, anamorphic flares. 2.39:1 for memory/drama.
- **THE POWER WORLD (Beverly Hills, CIA, the Oval Office, prison bureaucracy):** cold, clean, desaturated institutional blue-grey, clinical whites, hard shadows, locked-off tripod. Symmetry = control.

When the two worlds touch (Chico driving a Benz from Beverly Hills back to Compton), we **cross-fade the grade inside a single shot** — warm bleeds into cold. That transition IS the show's thesis, stated in color.

**Recurring visual motifs (seed in 101, pay off across the season):**
- **DICE** — chance, fate, "the game." Extreme macro, they tumble in slow-mo at every major decision.
- **RED & BLUE** — Bloods/Crips, but also police lights, but also the American flag. We rhyme all three.
- **THE FENCE / THE BRIDGE** — thresholds. Norman dies under a bridge; Chico's whole arc is "getting over the fence."
- **CHESS PIECES** — the memoir says homes were "scattered like chess pieces." Board imagery for the power game.
- **THE SHATTERED iPHONE** — the redemption payoff object; a cracked screen that films a Netflix deal.

**Archival treatment:** Reagan "Just Say No" broadcasts, LAPD Battering Ram footage, Iran-Contra hearings, NWA — all run through a CRT-scanline / VHS-tracking filter so era-footage reads instantly as "the record," distinct from our recreated cinematic footage.

## 6. SOUND & MUSIC IDENTITY

- **Score palette:** sub-bass drones + lone piano + G-funk synth whine (the Compton sound) + orchestral strings for grief. Composer reference: Ludwig Göransson meets DJ Quik.
- **Signature sound:** a single dice-rattle that hard-cuts to silence before every reveal. It becomes Pavlovian by episode 3 — audiences learn "rattle = truth incoming."
- **Needle-drops (era-authentic):** Toddy Tee "Batterram," N.W.A, Roger Troutman/Zapp talkbox. Use sparingly, license-aware; default to sound-alike cues where rights are open.
- **Narration:** Chico's own voice (first-person memoir). Warm, weathered, unhurried. He is the trusted guide who already survived it — dramatic irony fuels every scene.
- **Silence is a tool.** The three biggest reveals in the season play with NO music.

## 7. THE RETENTION SYSTEM (applies to every episode)

The **Hook → Curiosity → Reward → New Loop** engine, formalized:

1. **0:00–0:30 COLD OPEN HOOK** — the single most shocking image/claim of the episode, out of context.
2. **Open Loop A** immediately ("How did a kid from here end up there?").
3. **Micro-reward every 20–30s** — a fact lands, a scene turns, an image pays off.
4. **New micro-loop every reward** — never close a loop without opening the next.
5. **Act breaks are cliffhangers, not conclusions.**
6. **The episode's final 15 seconds** must make skipping to the next episode feel physically uncomfortable.

**Visual-change cadence:** something changes on screen every **5–10 seconds** (camera move, cut, graphic, light shift, text pop). No static talking-head longer than 8 seconds without a B-roll cutaway or push-in.
