## 3. Price positioning: anchor to test

Test **"Single items from $79. Full pickup load from $229."** with the $25 Neighborhood Special on top.

- $79 matches Dropcurb, sits under LoadUp's $85 national minimum and well under 1-800-GOT-JUNK's 1/8-truck minimum (about $100-$150 per [MoveBuddha](https://www.movebuddha.com/blog/got-junk-review/)). He shouldn't race LoadUp's $65. The pitch is a local owner who shows up, not the cheapest broker.
- $229 for a full pickup, or $204 after the $25, lands in the middle of the $120-$250 range. That's credible next to franchise 1/4-truck quotes ($130-$396).
- Don't discount the core price. The $25 stays a neighborhood or this-week reason to book, not a lower rate.
- **Measure:** run 2 weeks with the anchor on the funnel and in ads, then compare lead-to-booked rate against weeks without it. **Confirm with the owner first:** these are research-based suggestions, not his real costs. Dump fees at the local transfer station decide the floor.

## 4. His 64 reviews vs the field

- **Volume:** 64 at 5.0 in year one is about 5+ a month. The franchises show bigger totals (Junkluggers 557, College HUNKS 2,500+ self-reported), but those are multi-year and region-wide. Of the local independents, the only count found was Trash Talkers at 15 on HomeAdvisor. **Among owner-operators west of Downingtown, his count is likely top tier. I couldn't verify that because Google counts for Finks, JC and Absolute weren't findable.**
- **Threshold:** in BrightLocal's 2026 survey (as summarized by [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/reviews/blogs/online-review-statistics) and [Durable](https://durable.com/blog/importance-of-google-reviews)), 47% of consumers won't use a business with under 20 reviews and 31% want 4.5+ stars. He clears both easily.
- **Recency matters more than total:** the same summaries report 74% ignore reviews older than 3 months. Keep the pace at 5+ new reviews a month, every month. That's how a one-truck shop competes with a 557-review franchise.

## 5. Three local gaps to exploit

1. **Nobody local posts a price.** 1-800-GOT-JUNK, Junkluggers, JDog, College HUNKS and Junk King all quote on site. "Text a photo, get a price in 10 minutes" plus a "from $79" anchor is a real difference. **One audience:** Coatesville / Caln / Valley Twp homeowners. **One offer:** photo quote + $25 off. **One channel:** Meta Campaign A. **One measurable:** photo texts and form leads.
2. **Small jobs are a bad fit for big trucks.** The franchise truck holds about 7 pickup loads, and their minimum is a 1/8 truck. A single couch, a fridge or one garage corner is a perfect job for a Silverado. Target Facebook Marketplace sellers, curb-couch posts in local groups, and appliance swaps. **Measurable:** single-item bookings per week.
3. **He's the one who's actually close.** The franchise offices are in King of Prussia, Philadelphia and Sinking Spring. He's in 19320 and open 24 hours. If he can reliably come same day, say so (only then), and push the street-by-street Neighborhood blitz (Campaign B). Also make the Google Business Profile airtight: one phone number everywhere (the 245-4967 vs 209-9585 mismatch), every service listed, the website field filled, photos weekly. **Measurable:** GBP calls and direction taps from the Performance tab.
