## 6. Photos — the shot list

Ten to start, then a few every week. Recency is a live-business signal and it
gets you shown more.

- Logo (square, from `/brand/tb/png/icon-512.png`)
- Cover: a screen showing a site you built, well lit
- 3–4 before/after screenshots of client sites
- A screenshot of the AI agent mid-conversation
- A screenshot of a real analytics or leads dashboard
- You, working — a real photo, not stock
- Something recognisably Chester County in frame
- Any client business you have permission to show

Never stock photography. It reads as an agency that has nothing of its own.

## 8. Review requests — the texts

Send within an hour of the work going live, while the feeling is fresh.

**First ask:**
> Hey [name] — really glad we got [the site / the agent / the campaign] live for
> you. If it's worth 30 seconds, a quick Google review genuinely helps other
> local owners find us: [short link]. Either way, thanks for trusting me with
> it. — Nick

**Nudge, four days later, only if no review:**
> No pressure at all — just floating this back up in case it got buried:
> [short link]. Thanks [name].

**After a later win (a booked job, a good month):**
> That's great to hear. Any chance you'd put that in a Google review? It's the
> single most useful thing for a small shop like mine: [short link]

Target: **10 reviews in the first 60 days, then 3–5 a month, forever.** Rate and
recency beat raw volume in the medium term — which is how you get past a
competitor sitting on 309 reviews without waiting years.

---

## 9. Review replies

Reply to every one, within a day.

**Five stars:**
> Thanks [name] — glad [specific thing] is working for you. Shout any time you
> want to change something.

Name the specific thing. Generic thanks reads as automated and does nothing for
the person reading it later.

**Four stars:**
> Appreciate the honest review, [name]. If there's something that would have
> made it a five, tell me — I'd rather fix it than wonder.

**Negative:**
> [Name], I'm sorry — that's not the standard I want. [One sentence of what
> happened, no excuses.] I'd like to make it right; I'm at (484) 841-8501.

Never argue, never explain at length, never blame the customer. The reply is not
for the reviewer — it is for the next fifty people who read it, and a calm,
specific reply converts better than an unbroken row of fives.
